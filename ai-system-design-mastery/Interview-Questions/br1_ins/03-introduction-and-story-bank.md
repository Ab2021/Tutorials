# 03 — INTRODUCTION & STORY BANK

> **TL;DR.** Part 1 is the only part where you set the agenda, and it decides what they drill into for the next 20 minutes. So the intro is not a summary — it is a *steer*. This file gives you three intro lengths and a bank of 12 STAR stories mapped to the JD's competencies, so that any behavioural or "tell me about a time" question in the hour has a rehearsed answer that is 60 seconds long and ends in a number.

**60-second interview answer (the goal of this file):** *"Four sentences, one number, one hand-back question."* See §1.

---

## 1. The intro scripts

### 1.1 The 60-second version (default — memorise this shape)

| Beat | Sentence template | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | "I'm a [N]-year engineer, currently [role] at [company], where I lead [team size] people across [disciplines]." | Establishes seniority *immediately*. |
| **Proof** | "Most recently, [one-line project] — which [one number with a business meaning]." | The only hard evidence in the intro. |
| **Why Brit** | "The reason I applied is [Smarter Underwriting / Operational Simplification] — [one domain sentence showing you understand it]." | Proves you did homework, not spray-and-pray. |
| **Hand-back** | "That's the short version — would you like me to go deeper on the team, or on the most recent project?" | You lead; they choose the drill-down you have prepared. |

**Worked example (adapt heavily — do not claim what is not true):**

> *"I'm a 12-year engineer, currently leading a team of six engineers and scientists at [company], where we build document-AI and RAG systems for clinical operations. Most recently I led the build of a prior-authorisation intake pipeline that cut manual review time by 40% and put 30% of submissions through auto-adjudication. The reason I applied to Brit is the Smarter Underwriting agenda — submission triage is the same problem shape as clinical intake: semi-structured documents, a high cost for a wrong extraction, and a human who has to stay accountable for the decision. That's the short version — would you like me to go deeper on the team, or on that project?"*

### 1.2 The 2-minute version (only if they say "tell me more")

Sequence: **(1)** the arc — how you moved from your starting discipline into applied GenAI and why; **(2)** the team — size, disciplines, what they owned, what you owned; **(3)** one engineering standard you introduced; **(4)** why insurance/Lloyd's now. Then hand back.

**Do not** exceed two minutes. Time the recording.

### 1.3 The "why this role / why now" answer (they will ask)

Structure as **What attracted you → What you'd bring → What you want to build**:

> *"Three things. The problem — specialty insurance underwriting is genuinely document- and judgement-heavy, which is exactly where applied AI earns its keep rather than being a demo. The scope — the JD is explicit about both hands-on delivery and building a team, and that's the job I've actually been doing for the last two years. And the environment — I've worked in regulated settings where the model has to be explainable to an auditor, and I'd rather be somewhere that constraint is real than somewhere it isn't."*

### 1.4 Words to use and words to avoid in the intro

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "I led", "I owned", "we shipped", "[number]%" | "I was involved in", "I helped with", "we explored" |
| "Smarter Underwriting", "submission", "underwriter workbench" | "customer tickets", "users" generically |
| "regulated", "explainable", "human-in-the-loop" | "cutting-edge", "state of the art", "passionate" |
| "the trade-off was…" | "best practice" (unanchored) |

## 2. The story bank — 12 stories, mapped to the JD

Every behavioural question in the hour should be answerable from this bank. Write each as a **60-second STAR** with a *headline first*:

> **Headline:** *"The short answer is [conclusion]. Here's the situation…"*

Then S → T → A → R, and **always end with the number and the transferable lesson**.

| # | Story | JD competency it proves | Headline you should be able to say | Prep status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The project that shipped** | "ambiguous business problem to production operation" | "We took a vague ask and turned it into a production service that moved [metric] by [number]." | ☐ |
| 2 | **The decision that was non-obvious** | Technical judgement, trade-offs | "I chose the slower/cheaper/simpler option because [constraint], and here's what it bought us." | ☐ |
| 3 | **The thing that broke in production** | Production maturity, honesty | "It failed because [root cause]. The fix was [systemic change], not a patch." | ☐ |
| 4 | **The build-vs-buy call** | "pragmatic build-or-buy and pro-code or low-code decisions" | "We bought [tool] instead of building, because it wasn't our differentiator, and we spent the saved time on [X]." | ☐ |
| 5 | **The person you developed** | "mentor engineers… through pairing, technical reviews and stretch opportunities" | "I moved [X] from notebooks to owning a production service in two quarters, by [specific mechanism]." | ☐ |
| 6 | **The performance/behavioural conversation** | "address delivery or behavioural concerns constructively" | "I had a direct conversation early, with evidence, and the outcome was [Y]." | ☐ |
| 7 | **The conflict you resolved** | "resolve conflict fairly and promptly" | "Two teams disagreed on [X]; I got them to a decision by [mechanism], in [timeframe]." | ☐ |
| 8 | **The stakeholder you turned around** | "translate complex AI concepts into clear business choices" | "The business didn't trust the model, so I [approach], and they became the ones asking for the next use case." | ☐ |
| 9 | **The standard you set** | "set standards for architecture, code review, testing, CI/CD, observability, cost" | "I introduced [a specific gate], and it stopped [class of bug] reaching production." | ☐ |
| 10 | **The evaluation you built** | "evaluating AI system performance in real-world settings" | "I built a golden set of [N] cases that gates every deploy; here's what it caught." | ☐ |
| 11 | **The compliance/risk moment** | "security, privacy, data governance, model risk, human oversight" | "The model was ready but I held it back / added a human gate because [risk]." | ☐ |
| 12 | **The failure you own** | Self-awareness, no defensiveness | "I got [decision] wrong; the lesson changed how I run projects now." | ☐ |

### 2.1 Story quality rubric — score each of your 12 out of 5

| Criterion | 1 (weak) | 5 (strong) |
|---|---|---|
| Headline first | Starts mid-story | One-sentence conclusion, then evidence |
| Specificity | "We improved efficiency" | "We cut review time from 22 to 13 minutes" |
| Your action | "We decided" | "I proposed X, I ran the review, I made the call" |
| Trade-off named | No alternative mentioned | "We chose X over Y because Z; the cost was W" |
| Result + lesson | Result only | Result **and** the transferable principle |
| Length | > 2 min | 45–75 seconds |

**Rule:** any story scoring below 4 on "headline first" or "your action" **must be rewritten tonight**. Those two criteria sink candidates.

### 2.2 The three stories to over-prepare

If you only polish three, polish these — they map to the two *stated panel gaps* and to the project drill-down:

1. **#5 the person you developed** (mentoring gap)
2. **#8 the stakeholder you turned around** (business-integration gap)
3. **#1 the project that shipped** (the drill-down)

### 2.3 Three stories, fully worked (adapt to your truth — do not claim what is not true)

These are the three worth polishing most. Each is written to ~60 seconds, headline first, ending on a number and a principle. Use them as *shapes*; substitute your real facts.

**Story #1 — the project that shipped (the drill-down anchor).**

> **Headline:** *"We took a vague business ask and turned it into a production service that cut review time 40%."*
> **Situation:** *"The ask was 'make claims intake faster'. Nobody had measured where the time went."*
> **Task:** *"I was asked to own it end to end — discovery through production and the benefits review."*
> **Action:** *"First I shadowed three adjusters for a day and found 60% of the intake fields were already present verbatim in the submitted PDF, and the real cost was re-keying. So rather than build a chatbot, I built an extraction service: layout-aware parsing, a constrained schema with server-side validation, and a human queue for anything the validator rejected. I set the bar that every extracted field had to be traceable to a page and a box."*
> **Result:** *"Intake time per claim fell about 40%, and 30% of claims pre-populated end to end. The number that mattered to the business was cost per claim, not accuracy."*
> **Principle:** *"The lesson I'd carry: measure where the time actually goes before choosing the technology — the ask pointed at a chatbot and the data pointed at extraction."*

**Story #5 — the person you developed (closes the mentoring gap).**

> **Headline:** *"I moved a data scientist from notebooks to owning a production service in two quarters — by pairing, not by telling."*
> **Situation:** *"One of my strongest scientists produced excellent analysis but had never shipped anything; every model stayed in a notebook and died there."*
> **Task:** *"My goal wasn't to make her an engineer — it was to make her *independent* in production."*
> **Action:** *"I paired with her two hours a week, but I deliberately handed over the tools rather than the answers: she wrote the tests, she did the deploy, I sat on the rollback. I gave her one production service to own outright — smaller than her level, so she could succeed and then grow it."*
> **Result:** *"She now owns the extraction service and reviews other people's PRs. Two other people on the team followed the same path."*
> **Principle:** *"My rule is that nobody stays in notebooks — within a quarter, everyone has shipped something with a monitoring dashboard and their name on it. Ownership, not training courses, is what moves people."*

**Story #8 — the stakeholder you turned around (closes the business-integration gap).**

> **Headline:** *"The business didn't trust the model, so I stopped selling accuracy and sold traceability — and they became the ones asking for the next use case."*
> **Situation:** *"We'd shipped an extraction model that beat the old process on every metric, and the underwriters still double-checked every field. Adoption was near zero despite the numbers."*
> **Task:** *"The problem wasn't the model. It was that they couldn't see *why* it had produced a value, so they couldn't trust it."*
> **Action:** *"I added field-level provenance — every extracted value showed the page and the highlighted span it came from — and I published, honestly, where it was weak (handwritten endorsements). I stopped quoting accuracy at them and started showing them the source."*
> **Result:** *"Double-checking fell over about six weeks, and the same underwriters requested the next two use cases. Adoption was the metric, not F1."*
> **Principle:** *"Trust comes from traceability and honest limits, not from a better number. And overstating the model even once would have destroyed it permanently."*

### 2.4 The reusable scaffold (write your own in this shape)

```
HEADLINE   "The short answer is [conclusion]."
SITUATION  "The context was [X], and the problem was [Y] — worth [number]."
TASK       "I was asked to / I decided to [Z]."         (make your ownership explicit)
ACTION     "First I [did the diagnostic / asked the question]…
            then I [the decision — X over Y because Z]…
            and the hard part was [W]."
RESULT     "[Metric 1], [Metric 2]. The business number was [N]."
PRINCIPLE  "The lesson I'd carry forward is [transferable rule]."
```

**Timing check:** beat 1 ≈ 5 s, situation 10 s, task 5 s, action 20 s, result 10 s, principle 10 s = **~60 s**. If your action runs past 25 seconds you are telling the story chronologically instead of by decision — cut to the decision.

## 3. Domain-translation drills (for the "translate to business" requirement)

Practise saying each of these in **one underwriter-friendly sentence, with no jargon**:

| Technical term | Say this instead |
|---|---|
| Embedding / vector search | "It finds passages that *mean* the same thing, not just ones that share keywords — so 'water damage' matches 'flood ingress'." |
| RAG | "The model answers from *our* documents, and it shows you which document it used, so you can check it." |
| Hallucination | "The model can invent a plausible answer, so we only let it answer from retrieved evidence and we flag when it can't." |
| Precision vs recall | "Precision means when we say yes, it's right — it protects us from bad risk. Recall means we don't miss a good one — it protects the broker relationship." |
| Latency budget | "The underwriter will wait about 10 seconds for a full extraction, but a chat answer has to start appearing in under a second." |
| Fine-tuning | "Teaching the model a house style — it changes *how* it writes, not *what* it knows." |
| Guardrails | "Checks that the answer is safe and grounded before a human ever sees it." |
| Human-in-the-loop | "The model proposes; the underwriter decides. Nothing binds risk without a person." |

**Drill:** pick one from the left column at random and explain it in one sentence, out loud, in under 15 seconds. Do ten rounds.

## 4. Delivery mechanics (the `prev_learning` fixes, made concrete)

| Problem from the transcript | Mechanical fix | How to practise |
|---|---|---|
| "Uh", "like", filler | **Pause instead.** Count to two in your head before answering. | Record 5 answers; count fillers; re-record until zero. |
| Fragmented sentences | **Write only the first sentence** of each answer before you speak it. | Build a "first-sentence card" for your top 20 answers. |
| Theoretical drift | **Problem → Solution → Trade-off**, always. | Force every answer through the three words. |
| "I can't think of it" | **Bridge statement.** *"That's an interesting edge case — the first thing I'd pin down is X."* | Rehearse 5 bridges so they're automatic. |
| Fast, scattered pacing | **Slow to ~140 wpm.** A Lead sounds unhurried. | Use a metronome or a timer on a fixed passage. |
| Speech-to-text errors | **Headset + enunciate.** Part of the old grade was transcription quality. | Test-record on the same mic you'll use. |

## 5. Questions they will ask after the intro (pre-answer all five)

1. "Why are you leaving / why this role?" → §1.3
2. "How big was your team and what did you own?" → `12` §2
3. "What's the most complex AI system you've shipped?" → `04` §2 (90-second summary)
4. "What do you know about Brit?" → `01` §1, first three rows
5. "What do you want to do in the first 90 days?" → see below

**First-90-days answer (they ask this at Lead level):**

> *"Three phases. First month: listen — sit with underwriters, watch the actual submission flow, and map what's already in production versus in pilot. Second month: pick one use case with a clear owner and a measurable outcome, and take it end-to-end rather than starting three. Third: install one standard — I'd start with an evaluation gate in CI, because without it you can't safely ship anything afterwards."*

---

## 6. Cheat-sheet summary

- **Intro = 4 sentences, 1 number, 1 hand-back question.** 60 seconds max.
- **Steer the drill-down** by offering "the team, or the most recent project".
- **12 stories, 60 seconds each, headline first, number at the end.** Three are worked end to end in §2.3 — use them as scaffolds, then fill §2.4 with your own truth.
- **Over-prepare #1 (shipped project), #5 (mentoring), #8 (stakeholder).**
- **Translate one technical term per answer** — the JD scores this explicitly.
- **Pause instead of filling.** Zero filler words is the cheapest point available.
