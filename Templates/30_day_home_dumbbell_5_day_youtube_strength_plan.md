# 30-Day HOME Dumbbell Strength Plan — 5 Days/Week, YouTube Follow-Alongs

**7 November–6 December 2026 (India Standard Time) · Beginner-friendly modifications · At home · 10 kg dumbbell(s) · Garmin Forerunner 265**

> **Plan updated 7 November 2026.** This version replaces the earlier 3-day/week plan. **Every one of the 20 scheduled strength days includes TWO directly clickable YouTube videos:** a separate 5–15 minute **dynamic warm-up/stretch** first, and a **40-minute-or-longer dumbbell strength video** second. There are **5 strength days in each of four complete Saturday–Friday weeks**, plus two days for recovery and review.

## 1. At-a-glance weekly rhythm

| Day | Focus | Training effort | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saturday** | Full-body strength | Moderate, progressing gradually | Whole-body coverage |
| **Sunday** | Upper-body strength / technique | **Light–moderate** | Do not overload after Saturday |
| **Monday** | Lower-body strength | Moderate | Squat, hinge, glutes, balance |
| Tuesday | **Recovery, no lifting** | Easy / rest | Recover from three consecutive split days |
| **Wednesday** | Full-body strength | Moderate | Second complete-body exposure |
| **Thursday** | Lower-body + standing core **LIGHT** | **Easy–light** | Skill, trunk stability and low-fatigue practice |
| Friday | **Rest, no lifting** | Rest | Recover for next Saturday |

**Total:** 20 strength workout videos × 40 min minimum = **800+ minutes of advertised strength-video time**, plus **180 minutes of separate dynamic warm-up videos**, plus recovery. A 40-minute video includes its instructor’s warm-up, rest periods and/or cool-down where provided; it does **not** mean 40 uninterrupted minutes lifting 10 kg.

**Safety / workload qualification:** Five 40-minute *video sessions* per week are a fairly ambitious starting volume. To honour your preference without making five hard days, **Sunday and Thursday are deliberately lighter technique-strength days**. If you are new, take breaks or end early when form deteriorates. Prioritize safe movement over meeting a stopwatch target. ACSM emphasizes gradual, sustainable home-based resistance training; adults can gain benefits with even two strength days/week. See [sources](#10-sources-and-link-verification).

**Equipment:** one **10 kg** dumbbell *or* two 10 kg dumbbells, comfortable shoes, water, safe clear floor, optional mat/sturdy chair. **For most raises, curls, overhead presses and triceps moves 10 kg may be too heavy:** replace with **unweighted movement or 0.5–2 L water bottles**. In a video showing two weights, do the exercise one side at a time with the single weight **only if stable**, pausing as needed; otherwise use bottles/bodyweight.

## 2. Exact daily play order

On every **Strength** day: **(1)** clear floor / water / Garmin → **(2) click the warm-up video** and finish it → **(3) click the 40+ minute strength video** → **(4) record actual time and effort.** Do not replace the warm-up with long passive stretches before lifting; the warm-up videos use *dynamic* movement. Static stretching can be done after the workout.

1. **Preparation (2 min):** Check floor grip, drinking water, shoes, wall/chair stability; open the day’s **two YouTube links**.
2. **Record on Garmin:** Forerunner 265 → START → Strength → Free → START; for rapidly-changing timed videos, concentrate on recording total duration rather than manually confirming every set.
3. **Initial stretch/warm-up video:** Perform the *entire* linked **5-minute Juice & Toya** or **15-minute MadFit** routine **before lifting**. Both are dynamic and suitable for warm-up.
4. **Main workout video (≥40 min advertised):** Follow the trainer’s sequence but select **your own** resistance and tempo. Pause for water, rest, left/right symmetry. Even if the instructor uses two weights, **do not assume you must match them**.
5. **Intensity cap:** Week 1 RPE ~3–5; Week 2 RPE ~3–6; Week 3 RPE ~3–6; Week 4 RPE ~3–7. **Never try to make Thursday “hard”**. Aim to stop each set with 2–4 tidy reps still possible.
6. **Cool down (3–5 min):** Follow the video cool-down if provided, or gentle walking and comfortable static stretches after lifting (not before). Optional follow-along: [Caroline Girvan — UNWIND stretch and relax](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y87vSUoIMGU) (linked in her official training programme; length not specified here).
7. **Garmin notes (1–2 min):** Save the activity; record actual minutes, RPE and exercises done with 10 kg versus bottles/bodyweight.

### Movement-by-movement substitutions for a fixed 10 kg

| Shown on video | With one 10 kg DB | Safer alternative when 10 kg is too heavy |
|---|---|---|
| Goblet/sumo squat | One DB held centrally, only if steady | Chair squat / bodyweight squat |
| Romanian deadlift | One DB held with two hands or one hand (alternate sides) | Bodyweight hinge |
| Bent-over row | One-arm row with free hand on **stable** support | Water-bottle row |
| Lunge / split squat | Bodyweight first | Supported split squat / shorter range |
| Chest press | One-arm floor press **only if safely positioned and controlled** | Wall or incline push-up |
| Shoulder press / raise | 10 kg is often too heavy for a beginner | Unloaded reps or 0.5–2 L bottle |
| Curl / triceps exercise | Usually skip 10 kg if form suffers | One light bottle / unloaded reps |
| Row in plank / renegade row | Not required with a rolling dumbbell | Regular row + floor or wall plank |
| Two-dumbbell move | Pause and work one side at a time, if appropriate | Two light bottles / bodyweight |
| Combined squat-to-press / snatch | Separate squat and light press | Do the squat only; skip the explosive lift |

**Form first:** controlled descent, natural breathing (exhale during exertion), stable spine, no jerking, no painful range. Skip explosive/overhead complex movements until confident. Stop and seek medical care for chest pain, fainting, or concerning symptoms; seek guidance for injury or persistent pain.

## 5. Weekly progression that works without heavier dumbbells

| Week | Main days | Light days | What improves | Do **not** do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 kg only for comfortable squat/hinge/row | Bottles/bodyweight | Learn each movement; practice pausing | Force 10 kg on shoulders/curls |
| 2 | Better control and fewer unnecessary breaks | Keep light | Repeatable safe range and right/left balance | Add speed + resistance together |
| 3 | Optional 2–3 sec lowering phase on safe reps | Keep light | Time under tension and stable unilateral work | Lift to failure |
| 4 | Compare repeated videos at *same* load | Keep light | Good reps, confidence, consistency | Turn the final week into a max test |

**If you feel very sore or cannot maintain technique:** Convert the next lifting day into recovery; resume later, without doubling workouts. A scheduled 40-minute video is a **resource**, not a requirement to finish every rep under load. Two to three proper strength sessions are better than five unsafe ones. Five per week is your planned cadence, **not** a medical necessity.
