## 1. Purpose of this part

Parts VI teaches industries. This part teaches the **engineering disciplines that cut across all of
them**. A port, a hospital, a mine, and a retailer all have the same five problems underneath:

| Module | Discipline | The problem it solves |
|---|---|---|
| 41 | Operations engineering | Capacity is lumpy and shared; recovery is a scheduling problem |
| 42 | Financial engineering | The chain consumes cash before it produces it |
| 43 | Security engineering | The chain is an attack surface with physical, digital, and commercial doors |
| 44 | Platform engineering | A model that is not monitored is not a decision system |
| 45 | Facilities engineering | Design errors are cast in concrete |

Each module is 26 hours with a worked example, five labs, an assignment, a rubric, failure modes,
and a regulatory lens. All assume the core methods.

Module 20, provided the module-specific prerequisites are met.

## 4. What this part does not cover

- The commercial, financial-market, and legal advice that sits beside each discipline. Module 41
  excludes vessel and aircraft engineering; 42 excludes tax structuring and trading strategy; 43
  excludes offensive security work and classified programmes; 44 excludes cloud infrastructure
  engineering; 45 excludes structural and civil detailed design.
- The industry context the discipline operates in: see Part VI.
- The methods the discipline supports: see Part II.
