
## 5. Data, Tools & Method

### 5a. Data Specification

**Grain.** One row per ISO week (`week_start` Monday, business timezone), national aggregate,
all channels combined into a single row per channel-week. The analysis table is therefore
`weeks × channels` in long form, with 156 weeks and 3 channels, giving 468 channel-week rows
that are *not* independent — the 156 weeks are the effective sample, and every model
statement below treats the week as the unit of independent variation.

| Field | Type | Units | Definition | Availability at decision time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `week_start` | date | ISO Monday | Week key | Known |
| `channel` | enum | {A,B,C} | Paid search, paid social, programmatic display | Known |
| `spend` | decimal(12,2) | USD, platform-reported, excluding agency fee | Weekly spend invoiced by the platform | Known at week close + 3 business days |
| `impressions` | bigint | count | Platform-reported impressions | Known, but platform-attributed |
| `clicks` | bigint | count | Platform-reported clicks | Known, platform-attributed |
| `platform_conversions` | bigint | count | Conversions in the platform's own window | Known, with a 28-day maturity lag |
| `contribution` | decimal(14,2) | USD | Net revenue × 0.38 − media spend allocated to that channel-week | Known at week close + settlement lag |
| `price_index` | decimal(6,4) | ratio vs base | Catalogue price relative to the base basket | Known |
| `promo_week` | boolean | 0/1 | Whether a sitewide promotion ran | Known |
| `holiday_weeks_to_peak` | int | weeks | Signed distance to the winter peak week | Known |

**Outcome contract.** The modelled outcome is `contribution`, defined as net revenue after
returns and discounts times the blended contribution rate of 0.38, minus the media spend
allocated to that channel. The subtraction of spend matters: it makes the response curve's
contribution value a *net* contribution, so a marginal return of 0.5 means fifty cents of net
margin per additional dollar, not $1.50 of gross margin. Getting this convention wrong
double-counts the cost of media and makes every channel look unprofitable. This is stated once
here and used consistently: **all contribution figures in this case are net of the spend that
produced them.**

**Population and window.** All weeks from 2022-W40 to 2025-W38 inclusive. The first 130 weeks
are the estimation window; the final 26 weeks are held out. Within the estimation window, the
last 13 weeks form a validation fold used for early selection, and the held-out 26 weeks are
never used for any fitting decision.

**Time semantics.** Three clocks appear and are never interchanged. *Event time* is when the
customer act occurred. *Reporting time* is when the platform recorded it (platform conversion
windows extend up to 28 days, so a week's platform conversions can still change for four
weeks). *Decision time* is the Monday on which the budget is set. The model uses settled
contribution with a 35-day lag, which means the most recent three to four weeks are always
incomplete and are excluded from estimation. Anyone comparing a model output to a live
dashboard in the same week is comparing settled data to unsettled data.

**Denominators.** Every rate in this case names its denominator. Return on spend is
`contribution / spend`, both in USD over the same channel-week. Marginal return is
`d(contribution)/d(spend)`, dimensionless because both numerator and denominator are USD. The
calibration factor is a ratio of two lift percentages and is dimensionless. The case never
mixes a share-of-credit with a rate.

**Illustrative series summary.** Because the series is constructed, its summary statistics are
stated so the arithmetic downstream can be checked rather than trusted.

| Statistic | Channel A | Channel B | Channel C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean weekly spend | $40,000 | $30,000 | $10,000 |
| Spend standard deviation | $9,800 | $12,400 | $2,900 |
| Weeks at zero spend | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Correlation of spend with A | 1.000 | 0.71 | 0.34 |
| Mean settled contribution | $52,462 | $25,412 | $8,266 |

The 0.71 correlation between A and B is the identification problem in one number. When two
spend series move together for 130 weeks, a regression can trade their effects off against each
other almost freely, and the prior on the saturation shape becomes the binding constraint on
the split of credit. That is why the refresh's improvement in holdout fit does not certify the
channel-level returns.

**Exclusions.** Weeks with a sitewide promotion are retained but flagged; the model includes
the flag rather than dropping the weeks, because promotional weeks are 19% of the series and
dropping them would remove most of the high-spend variation that identifies the upper part of
each curve — the exact region the allocation decision depends on. Two weeks with a platform
billing correction are excluded from the estimation window and listed in the exclusion ledger;
their spend was restated, and using the original figure would inject a spurious spend spike.

### 5b. Tools & Stack

| Layer | Choice | Why this and not the alternative | Failure boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storage | Columnar warehouse table, weekly partition | Weekly grain is small; no need for a streaming store | Late-arriving restatements require a re-partition |
| Transformation | Versioned SQL models with tests on row counts, null rates, and monotone spend | Reproducibility; the audit requirement is a frozen version | Tests do not validate semantics, only shape |
| Estimation | Bayesian regression with informative priors on saturation shape, geometric adstock | A 156-week series cannot identify flexible curves; priors make the fit stable | Priors encode beliefs, so fit quality is not evidence of correctness |
| Optimisation | Deterministic grid search at $500 resolution plus analytic marginal check | Grid is transparent and its resolution error is computable; the analytic derivative verifies it | Grid misses interior optima finer than $500 |
| Sensitivity | Parametric re-run over a stated grid of calibration factors and decay parameters | Cheap; exposes which decisions are fragile | Does not propagate full posterior correlation |
| Orchestration | Weekly scheduled job, manual trigger for the refresh | Low cadence; no streaming requirement | A failed run silently leaves last week's numbers in place unless checked |
| Reporting | Frozen notebook export plus a decision record | The audit trail is the artefact, not the dashboard | Dashboards show live unsettled data |

The tool choices are deliberately boring. The risk in this case is not computational; it is
that a well-fitted model is asked a question outside its support. A more sophisticated
estimator would not have caught the problem — the calibration check did.

### 5c. Method Summary

The method has five steps, each with a stated input and output.

**Step 1 — Transform spend into effective exposure.** For channel $c$ and week $t$, geometric
adstock with decay $\lambda_c$ produces an effective spend stock. Validity: the decay is
constant over the window, spend is non-negative, and carryover does not cross a structural
break such as a channel launch.

```text
FORMULA [MMM-01]: a_{c,t} = x_{c,t} + lambda_c * a_{c,t-1},  a_{c,0} = x_{c,0}
WHERE: a_{c,t} effective spend stock [USD/week]; x_{c,t} observed spend [USD/week];
       lambda_c carryover retention [dimensionless, 0 <= lambda_c < 1]; t weeks.
CONSTRAINT: constant lambda; x >= 0; stationary start; no channel relaunch inside window.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: x = 40,000 every week, lambda_A = 0.6:
       a_1 = 40,000; a_2 = 40,000 + 0.6*40,000 = 64,000;
       steady state a_inf = 40,000/(1-0.6) = 100,000.
INTERPRETATION: at constant spend the stock settles at 2.5x the weekly flow, so the curve is
       evaluated at an effective level far above the observed weekly spend.
```

Derivation: repeated substitution of the recursion gives
$a_{c,t} = \sum_{k \ge 0} \lambda_c^k x_{c,t-k}$, which converges for $|\lambda_c|<1$ and equals
$x/(1-\lambda_c)$ under constant input. The half-life follows from $\lambda^{h}=0.5$.

```text
FORMULA [MMM-02]: h_c = ln(0.5) / ln(lambda_c)
WHERE: h_c half-life [weeks]; lambda_c retention per week [dimensionless].
CONSTRAINT: 0 < lambda_c < 1.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: lambda_A=0.6 -> h_A = ln0.5/ln0.6 = -0.6931/-0.5108 = 1.357 weeks;
       lambda_B=0.3 -> h_B = 0.576 weeks; lambda_C=0.4 -> h_C = 0.756 weeks.
INTERPRETATION: these are fast decays — most of a week's effect lands within two weeks — so
       the model is effectively a short-window response model, not a brand-equity model.
```

**Step 2 — Map effective exposure to contribution with a Hill saturation.** The Hill form is
chosen over an exponential because it has a shape parameter that lets the curve be S-shaped,
which matters when small spends have little effect and mid spends have a steep region.

```text
FORMULA [MMM-03]: H_c(a) = a^s_c / (a^s_c + K_c^s_c)
WHERE: H dimensionless saturation in [0,1); a effective stock [USD/week];
       s shape [dimensionless, >0]; K half-saturation stock [USD/week] where H=0.5.
CONSTRAINT: a >= 0; s > 0; K > 0; H -> 1 as a -> infinity.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: a=100,000, s=1.5, K=80,000:
       a^s = 100000^1.5 = 3.1623e7; K^s = 80000^1.5 = 2.2627e7;
       H = 3.1623e7/(3.1623e7+2.2627e7) = 3.1623/5.4250 = 0.5829.
INTERPRETATION: 58.3% of the channel's maximum contribution is realised at this stock.
```

Contribution for channel $c$ is $\beta_c H_c(a_c)$, where $\beta_c$ is the saturation ceiling
in USD/week. The ceiling is the parameter most exposed to confounding: it absorbs the average
level of everything correlated with the channel, including demand that would have occurred
without the spend.

**Step 3 — Estimate.** Bayesian regression on the 130 estimation weeks with normal priors on
$\beta$, log-normal priors on $K$, and a half-normal prior on $s$ centred at 1.4. The priors
are not decoration: the likelihood surface over $(s, K)$ is nearly flat for channel C because
its spend range is narrow ($2,900 standard deviation on a $10,000 mean), so a flat prior
produces a curve whose marginal return at the operating point is essentially arbitrary.

**Step 4 — Evaluate out of sample.** Predict the 26 held-out weeks using only observed spend
and controls, then compute mean absolute percentage error, root mean squared error, and mean
signed percentage error. The signed error is the one that matters for allocation, because it
tests *level*, not just shape.

**Step 5 — Calibrate and re-optimise.** Compare the model's implied lift for channel B over
the geo-test window with the geo test's measured lift, form the ratio, apply the ratio to
$\beta_B$ only, and re-solve the constrained allocation. Section 6 executes all five steps
with numbers.

### 5d. Provenance, Quality, Exclusions and Leakage

**Provenance.** The dataset is constructed. Its structure mirrors what a real weekly mart
would contain, and its field definitions are stated so that a reader can substitute a real
series without changing the method. No vendor pricing, no platform rate card, and no company
volume is represented. The geo-test readings in Section 6 are constructed readings with a
construction that mimics a matched-metro design; they are not a published test.

**Quality checks that were specified (not executed against a real mart).**

| Check | Expected | Failure action |
|---|---|---|
| Row count per week = 3 channels | Exactly 3 | Block the refresh; a missing channel shifts the envelope |
| Spend non-negative and below $250,000/week | True | Investigate; a restated invoice can breach this legitimately |
| Contribution reconciles to finance ledger within 0.5% | True on 12 sampled weeks | Hold the refresh; the outcome definition has drifted |
| Platform conversions monotone non-decreasing for 28 days after week close | True | If a week's conversions fall, the platform restated; exclude the week |
| No week with simultaneous A and B spend change > 60% | True | Such weeks dominate identification and can be a campaign launch, not a budget decision |

**Exclusions and their consequence.** Two billing-correction weeks are excluded. The
consequence is stated rather than waved away: excluding them removes the two largest
single-week spend jumps in the series, which slightly *reduces* the identified steepness of
the search curve. A model estimated with those weeks included would report a marginally higher
marginal return for search, which would favour search more strongly — the direction of the
exclusion's effect is therefore known, and it does not change the decision because the
decision turns on channel B's calibration, not on A's steepness.

**Leakage.** Three leakage channels were checked.

1. *Outcome leakage.* Contribution for week $t$ includes revenue from orders placed in $t$ but
   settled later. Using the latest available figure for a held-out week would use information
   not available at that week's decision time. The holdout therefore uses contribution as
   settled 35 days after week close, identically to the estimation window.
2. *Feature leakage.* The `holiday_weeks_to_peak` feature is deterministic from the calendar,
   so it is available at decision time. The `promo_week` flag is planned in advance and is
   available. Platform conversions are **not** used as a feature because their 28-day window
   overlaps the outcome and would leak outcome information into the input.
3. *Selection leakage.* The model specification (prior centres, curve family) was fixed before
   the holdout was examined. The one exception is disclosed: the early-stopping fold inside the
   estimation window was used to choose between geometric and delayed adstock, and geometric
   won. That choice consumed 13 weeks of information, which is why the reported holdout is 26
   weeks rather than 39.

**A quality problem that was found and kept visible.** The first refresh attempt reported a
search ceiling $\beta_A$ of $112,000/week, up from $90,000 in `v1`. That jump is not credible
on a channel whose spend did not change materially. The cause was a change in how returns were
allocated to channel in the mart: the previous version allocated marketplace fee revenue to
search, the new version allocated it to display. The correction re-ran the refresh on a
consistent allocation. The lesson is recorded because it is the most common real cause of a
"model changed its mind" surprise: the outcome definition moved.

### 5e. Reproducibility and Method-Selection Ledger

**Reproducibility contract.** A reader must be able to reproduce every number in Section 6
from the parameters in this subsection. The frozen inputs are: the three parameter sets below,
the adstock recursion, the Hill form, the budget and constraint values, and the holdout series.
No random seed is required because all computations are deterministic; the "Bayesian" fit is
represented here by its posterior point estimates, which is a simplification stated openly — a
real implementation would report posterior intervals, and this case instead reports a
parametric sensitivity grid, which is a weaker but transparent substitute.

```text
Channel parameter sets used throughout Section 6 (ILLUSTRATIVE posterior point estimates)
A  paid search : lambda=0.60, s=1.5, K=80,000, beta=90,000
B  paid social : lambda=0.30, s=2.0, K=50,000, beta=60,000
C  display     : lambda=0.40, s=1.2, K=30,000, beta=25,000
Budget = 80,000/week. Floors: C >= 10,000; B >= 8,000. Ceiling: C <= 20,000.
Current plan: A=40,000, B=30,000, C=10,000.
```

**Method-selection ledger.** Each rejected alternative is recorded with the reason and the
condition under which it would have won.

| Decision | Chosen | Rejected alternative | Why rejected here | When the alternative wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curve family | Hill with shape | Linear-in-log (power) | Power form cannot express a threshold region, and C's data has one | When spend range is wide and monotone response is credible |
| Adstock | Geometric | Delayed adstock (peak-shifted) | Geometric won on the 13-week early-stop fold | When there is a measurable lag between exposure and response, e.g. offline media |
| Estimation | Bayesian with informative priors | OLS with regularisation | OLS produced sign flips between refits on A/B collinearity | When spend series are near-orthogonal and n is large |
| Validation | 26-week holdout plus geo calibration | Holdout only | Holdout tests reproduction of history, not level under a new policy | When the model is used only for forecasting, not allocation |
| Calibration | Scalar factor on $\beta_B$ | Re-estimation with the geo data as a likelihood term | Scalar is transparent and its failure is bounded; joint re-estimation would let six weeks dominate 130 | When the geo test is long, multi-channel, and covers the planning spend range |
| Optimisation | $500 grid plus analytic derivative check | Continuous gradient solver | Grid error is computable and small relative to parameter uncertainty | When the feasible set is high-dimensional |

The last row of that ledger is the honest statement of this case's central limitation: the
grid resolution is *not* the binding uncertainty. Parameter uncertainty and identification
bias are two to three orders of magnitude larger, which is why Section 9 spends its effort on
calibration sensitivity rather than on optimiser precision.
