> **Evidence:** constructed teaching scenario; no carrier, route, or measured result is asserted.

## 1. The One-Line Framing
Given a dated set of delivery stops, choose feasible vehicle routes for the dispatch lead to reduce distance subject to vehicle capacity, service windows, and driver-shift limits, compared with manual geographic batching.
**Takeaway:** objective improvement never compensates for an infeasible route.

## 5. Data, Tools & Method
### 5a. Data Specification
One row is a stop with demand, service window, service minutes, and travel-time scenario known at dispatch cut.
### 5b. Tools & Stack
Proposed route matrix, feasibility checker, exact small-instance oracle, heuristic solver, and dispatcher override ledger.
### 5c. Method Summary
Construct feasible routes, compare manual batches with a capacity/time-window heuristic, and send only feasible plans to dispatch.
### 5d. Provenance, quality, exclusions and leakage controls
Travel estimates and cancellation events are versioned by availability time; future arrival observations cannot enter dispatch-time validation.
### 5e. Reproducibility ledger
Illustrative inputs and arithmetic are reproducible; operational source evidence is OPEN.

## 6. Analysis Walkthrough
```text
FORMULA [ROUTE-01]: C = sum(demand_i) <= Q
WHERE: C route load [parcels]; Q vehicle capacity [parcels].
CONSTRAINT: all assigned stops must meet both capacity and time-window feasibility.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: 18+12+20=50 parcels; Q=52, so the capacity constraint has 2 parcels slack.
INTERPRETATION: a shorter route that carries 53 parcels is infeasible.
```
Sensitivity: if demand at the third stop rises from 20 to 23, load becomes 53 and the route must be split or reassigned.
