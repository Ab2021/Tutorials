## 5. Worked Numerical Example

**Evidence label.** The eight-request job set, its values, and its durations are **ILLUSTRATIVE** construction. The arithmetic is VERIFIED here in two independent ways: by the weighted-interval recurrence and by exhaustive enumeration of all `2^8 = 256` subsets with a pairwise-compatibility filter. Where the two disagree the enumeration is authoritative, because it shares no code with the recurrence.

**The scenario.** One test bay handles eight service requests in a twenty-hour window. The commercial question is which requests to accept when not all can be served. Three policy questions arrive from three different owners, and they have three different answers, which is the point of the exercise:

- (a) operations: "fit as many as you can" — the count objective;
- (b) commercial: "maximise booked value" — the additive value objective;
- (c) facilities: "what does a one-hour turnaround between jobs cost us?" — the buffer sensitivity.

**Input ledger.** Times in hours from the start of the window; value in abstract value units; all intervals half-open `[s, f)`.

| Id | `s` | `f` | Duration | `w` | `w / duration` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0 | 6 | 6 | 5 | 0.8333 |
| B | 4 | 6 | 2 | 3 | 1.5000 |
| C | 4 | 10 | 6 | 8 | 1.3333 |
| D | 6 | 12 | 6 | 6 | 1.0000 |
| E | 8 | 14 | 6 | 4 | 0.6667 |
| F | 10 | 16 | 6 | 9 | 1.5000 |
| G | 12 | 18 | 6 | 5 | 0.8333 |
| H | 14 | 20 | 2 | 2 | 1.0000 |

Total busy time is `6+2+6+6+6+6+6+2 = 40` hour-units across a horizon of `20` hours, giving a volume lower bound of `ceil(40/20) = 2` resources. Wait — recompute: `6 + 2 = 8`, `+6 = 14`, `+6 = 20`, `+6 = 26`, `+6 = 32`, `+6 = 38`, `+2 = 40`. So `40` hour-units, and `40/20 = 2.0` exactly. The peak simultaneous depth, computed by sweeping `+1` at each start and `-1` at each finish, is `3`: at hour `10`, requests `B` and `C` have finished, `D` is open on `[6,12)`, `E` is open on `[8,14)`, and `F` opens at `10`, so `D`, `E`, `F` are simultaneously live. The volume bound `2` is therefore **not** tight: `ceil(40/20) = 2` while the true requirement is `3`. That gap between the volume bound and the peak is itself one of the catalogue issues (CI-014).

**Finish-sorted working index.** Sort by `(f, s, id)`:

| `i` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| id | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H |
| `s_i` | 0 | 4 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 |
| `f_i` | 6 | 6 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 |
| `w_i` | 5 | 3 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 9 | 5 | 2 |

`A` and `B` tie at `f = 6`; the tie is broken by start time (`0 < 4`). This choice is declared, not derived, and CI-022 in the catalogue shows what happens when two builds declare it differently.

**Predecessors with `g = 0`.** `p(i)` is the largest `j < i` with `f_j <= s_i`.

| `i` | id | `s_i` | finishes `<= s_i` | `p(i)` |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | A | 0 | none | 0 |
| 2 | B | 4 | none (`f_1 = 6 > 4`) | 0 |
| 3 | C | 4 | none (`f_1 = 6 > 4`) | 0 |
| 4 | D | 6 | `f_1 = 6`, `f_2 = 6` | 2 |
| 5 | E | 8 | up to `f_2 = 6` | 2 |
| 6 | F | 10 | up to `f_3 = 10` | 3 |
| 7 | G | 12 | up to `f_4 = 12` | 4 |
| 8 | H | 14 | up to `f_5 = 14` | 5 |

**First derivation — the value recurrence.** `OPT(i) = max(OPT(i-1), w_i + OPT(p(i)))` with `OPT(0) = 0`:

```text
i=1  A  take 5 + OPT(0) = 5    vs skip OPT(0) = 0     -> 5
i=2  B  take 3 + OPT(0) = 3    vs skip 5              -> 5
i=3  C  take 8 + OPT(0) = 8    vs skip 5              -> 8
i=4  D  take 6 + OPT(2) = 11   vs skip 8              -> 11
i=5  E  take 4 + OPT(2) = 9    vs skip 11             -> 11
i=6  F  take 9 + OPT(3) = 17   vs skip 11             -> 17
i=7  G  take 5 + OPT(4) = 16   vs skip 17             -> 17
i=8  H  take 2 + OPT(5) = 13   vs skip 17             -> 17
```

`OPT(8) = 17`. Reconstruction from `i = 8`: `H` is skipped (`13 < 17`), `G` is skipped (`16 < 17`), `F` is taken (`9 + OPT(3) = 9 + 8 = 17` equals the table entry and exceeds `OPT(5) = 11`), so jump to `p(6) = 3`; at `i = 3`, `C` is taken (`8 + 0 = 8` exceeds `OPT(2) = 5`), jump to `0`. Accepted set `{C, F}`, occupying `[4,10)` and `[10,16)`, value `8 + 9 = 17`, and the bay is idle on `[0,4)` and `[16,20)`.

**Independent re-check by enumeration.** Of the `256` subsets, `31` are pairwise compatible at `g = 0`. The maximum value among them is `17`, attained by `{C, F}` alone. The maximum *cardinality* is `3`, attained by six sets — `{A,D,G}` 16, `{B,D,G}` 14, `{A,D,H}` 13, `{B,D,H}` 11, `{A,E,H}` 11, `{B,E,H}` 9 — so cardinality is a weak objective: it cannot distinguish six schedules whose values span `9` to `16`. So the value optimum and the count optimum are **different sets on this instance**, and the count-optimal set is worth `1` less, a loss of `(17 - 16)/17 = 5.88%`. That is the smallest version of the objective confusion in the catalogue, and it is already enough to make the reported number wrong.

**Second derivation — count greedy.** Scan in finish order, keeping `L` = the finish of the last accepted job:

```text
i=1 A  s=0  >= L=-inf  accept  L=6
i=2 B  s=4  >= 6? no   reject
i=3 C  s=4  >= 6? no   reject
i=4 D  s=6  >= 6? yes  accept  L=12
i=5 E  s=8  >= 12? no  reject
i=6 F  s=10 >= 12? no  reject
i=7 G  s=12 >= 12? yes accept  L=18
i=8 H  s=14 >= 18? no  reject
```

Accepted `{A, D, G}`, count `3`, value `16`. Enumeration confirms `3` is the maximum count. This is the correct algorithm for question (a) and it answers question (b) with a number that is `1` unit (`5.88%`) short — and nothing in the output says so.

**Third derivation — the density heuristic, which looks right and is not.** Sort by value per unit duration descending: `B` and `F` both `1.5000`, `C` `1.3333`, `D` and `H` `1.0000`, `A` and `G` `0.8333`, `E` `0.6667`. With `F` before `B` by the declared id tie-break, take greedily:

```text
F [10,16)  accept                       value 9
B [4,6)    compatible with F  accept    value 12
C [4,10)   conflicts with B             reject
D [6,12)   conflicts with F             reject
H [14,20)  conflicts with F             reject
A [0,6)    conflicts with B             reject
G [12,18)  conflicts with F             reject
E [8,14)   conflicts with F             reject
```

Accepted `{B, F}`, value `12`, against the optimum `17`: a loss of `(17 - 12)/17 = 29.41%`. This is the heuristic an experienced-sounding person proposes first ("go where the value per hour is highest"), and it is worse than the naive count greedy's `16`. Sorting by raw value descending instead gives `{F, C}` immediately — `F` taken, `C` compatible with `F` because `10 <= 10` — worth `17`, the true optimum, by luck rather than by design. Two plausible heuristics, one `29.41%` short and one exactly optimal, and **the output cannot tell you which happened**. That is why the catalogue insists on an oracle rather than on plausibility.

**Fourth derivation — the buffer.** Recompute with `g = 1`, i.e. `f_j + 1 <= s_i`:

| `i` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `p_g(i)` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| `OPT_g(i)` | 5 | 5 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 14 | 14 | 14 |

Entry checks: `i = 4` (`D`, `s = 6`): `f_1 + 1 = 7 <= 6` is false, so `p_g(4) = 0` and take `6 + 0 = 6` against skip `OPT_g(3) = 8` → `8`. `i = 6` (`F`, `s = 10`): `f_3 + 1 = 11 <= 10` false, `f_2 + 1 = 7 <= 10` true, so `p_g(6) = 2`; take `9 + OPT_g(2) = 9 + 5 = 14` against skip `9` → `14`. `i = 8` (`H`, `s = 14`): `f_5 + 1 = 15 <= 14` false, `f_4 + 1 = 13 <= 14` true, so `p_g(8) = 4`; take `2 + OPT_g(4) = 2 + 8 = 10` against skip `14` → `14`.

`OPT_g(8) = 14`, attained by `{A, F}` (`5 + 9 = 14`), confirmed by enumeration: `20` compatible subsets, maximum value `14`, maximum cardinality `2` (`{A, E}`). The one-hour turnaround costs `(17 - 14)/17 = 17.65%` of value and drops the maximum count from `3` to `2`, a `33.33%` reduction. Note that `g = 2` gives the identical answer — the policy change from `1` to `2` hours is free on this instance because no accepted pair sits within `2` hours of a boundary. **A sensitivity table that only probes `g = 0` and `g = 1` would therefore overstate the marginal cost of the policy and understate the range over which it is free.**

**Independent arithmetic re-check.** Three checks that do not reuse the recurrence:

1. *Value from the accepted list.* `8 + 9 = 17` equals `OPT(8) = 17`. For the buffer case, `5 + 9 = 14` equals `OPT_g(8) = 14`.
2. *Pairwise replay.* `{C, F}`: `f_C = 10 <= s_F = 10` ✓. `{A, F}`: `f_A = 6`, `s_F = 10`, `6 + 1 = 7 <= 10` ✓. `{A, D, G}`: `6 <= 6` ✓, `12 <= 12` ✓.
3. *Monotonicity in the buffer.* Every set feasible at `g = 1` is feasible at `g = 0`, so `OPT_g <= OPT` is required: `14 <= 17` ✓. Had this failed, the predecessor array would have been stale.

**Sensitivity note.** The value optimum is remarkably flat in the buffer: `17`, `14`, `14` for `g = 0, 1, 2`, and it stays at `14` for `g = 1, 2, 3, 4` before falling to `10` at `g = 5` (the accepted pair `{A, F}` has a `4`-hour gap `[6,10)`, so any buffer up to `4` is free and a buffer of `5` breaks the pair). The count optimum is not flat: `3` then `2`, and it cannot recover. So the correct statement to the facilities owner is not "a one-hour turnaround costs 17.65%" but "a turnaround of up to four hours costs 17.65% and stays flat there, and only at five hours does the plan lose another 28.57% of its value, falling from 14 to 10" — a materially different commitment, and one that a single-point sensitivity cannot produce.

**What this example does not establish.** It does not establish that additive value is the right objective, that value units are comparable across request types, that eight requests are representative, or that the bay has zero changeover beyond the declared buffer. It establishes only that for a stated model the arithmetic is correct, that three reasonable objectives give three different accepted sets from one input, and that two plausible heuristics land `29.41%` apart with no visible signal of which is which.

**Takeaway.** The instance's four numbers — `17` (value optimum), `16` (count optimum), `14` (value optimum under a one-hour buffer), `12` (density heuristic) — differ by amounts large enough to change a commercial decision, and no feasibility check, no solver status line, and no exception separates them.
