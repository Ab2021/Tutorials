# 9.3 — Evaluation Benchmarks: Comprehensive Design

> **Course location:** Module 28, AI Evaluation, Monitoring and Continuous Improvement. This lesson maps to `finetuning_course/Module_09_Evaluation_Debugging/9.3_Evaluation_Benchmarks_Comprehensive.md` in [`Train/Templates/directory.txt`](../../../Templates/directory.txt). The source lesson body was unavailable; this is a new mapped draft, not a recovered lesson.
>
> **Boundary:** A benchmark is a measurement protocol: task population, unit, prompt, assets, tool state, scoring, slices, versions and decision rule. A leaderboard score or test-suite pass alone does not establish production fitness.

## Learning outcomes

Learners can construct a versioned benchmark manifest; separate construct validity, contamination and scoring reliability; calculate micro and macro slice summaries; state aggregation weights and coverage; design a frozen comparison with critical-failure gates; and refuse a release claim when the test population or evidence cannot support it.

## 1. Start from the deployment decision

A “comprehensive” benchmark is not the largest collection of public tasks. It is a bounded suite that provides enough evidence for a specific decision. Define:

- intended users, requests, languages/domains, access conditions and consequence of error;
- unit of evaluation: request, document, conversation, tool trajectory, user, organization or time period;
- correct/acceptable/unsafe/abstain outcomes and who adjudicates ambiguity;
- reference baseline and candidate versions, decoding/tool/retrieval settings and resource limits;
- primary metric, required slices, critical failure definitions, uncertainty rule and promotion/rollback owner;
- exclusions and claims the suite cannot support.

A generic knowledge benchmark may be useful as a regression signal but cannot prove quality on private workflows. A production replay can be relevant but may be contaminated by prior model outputs, selected traffic or mutable tool state. Use both only with their limitations explicit.

## 2. Benchmark manifest and provenance

Treat the manifest as an immutable release input. Minimum fields:

| Field | Requirement | Failure if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Suite/item IDs and version hash | Immutable item content and order or explicit seed | Result cannot be reproduced |
| Task and scoring contract | Input, allowed output, reference/rubric, unit and metric | “Correct” is ambiguous |
| Population/slices | Eligibility and target/dependency boundaries | Aggregate cannot support intended claim |
| Prompt/template and decoding | Exact system/prompt, tools, seed/temperature and max tokens | Candidate comparison is confounded |
| Assets/tool state | Retrieved corpus, sandbox image, API versions and time | Tool result is non-reproducible |
| Candidate/baseline identity | Immutable model, tokenizer, adapter, runtime and config | Score lacks lineage |
| Contamination review | Source/provenance and matching method/results | Test may measure memorization |
| Exceptions | Timeouts, refusals, missing tools, invalid outputs and scorer errors | Failures disappear from denominator |
| Approval/gates | Metric/slice thresholds, uncertainty and release owner | Score cannot drive a decision |

Keep raw outputs and scoring traces subject to privacy/security policy. A metric without the prompt/tool/result record cannot be audited when a high-stakes failure appears.

## 3. Aggregation makes a value judgment

For disjoint slices `s`, correct count `c_s` and eligible count `n_s`, micro accuracy is:

```text
FORMULA [BENCH-01]: micro_accuracy = sum_s c_s / sum_s n_s
                    macro_accuracy = (1/S) * sum_s (c_s/n_s)
WHERE: S is number of included slices; c_s and n_s use the same eligibility
       and correctness contract; each n_s > 0 for macro accuracy.
CONSTRAINT: report slice counts and selected aggregation; no slice is silently
            omitted after observing its result.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: slice A has 90/100 correct; slice B has 4/10 correct.
  micro_accuracy=94/110=0.8545454545; macro_accuracy=(.90+.40)/2=.65.
INTERPRETATION: the high-volume slice dominates micro accuracy; macro gives the
                two declared slices equal weight. Neither is a neutral default.
```

Weighted averages are valid only after weights are predeclared from a decision rule, such as expected traffic or harm-weighted priorities. They must not be tuned after seeing scores to make one candidate win. Report both the decision statistic and raw slices; a critical slice may be a hard gate rather than an average term.

Coverage is separate: `coverage = eligible_scored / eligible_target`. A high score on an unrepresentative or selectively dropped subset is not a high-quality result. Report item counts through every exclusion: proposed, ineligible by policy, execution failure, scorer failure, valid scored and critical failures.

## 4. Contamination and leakage controls

Contamination means the evaluation result can be inflated because test content, answers, near duplicates or a dependent artifact entered training, tuning, retrieval or prompt construction. It is not established merely because a model performs well; it is also not ruled out by a simple exact-string search.

Use layered controls:

1. **Provenance:** record item author/source/date/license, transformations, answer generation and exposure paths.
2. **Dependency splits:** separate by document family, repository, user, organization, time or template when those units can leak shared information.
3. **Candidate corpus audit:** compare evaluation inputs/answers against authorized train, fine-tune, retrieval and prompt assets using exact, normalized and semantic/near-duplicate candidates; route candidates to human review.
4. **Fresh/private holdout:** where feasible, create post-freeze items unavailable to model-development access paths.
5. **Disclosure:** state search coverage, match thresholds, false-positive handling, unresolved items and what cannot be audited (for example, opaque pretraining data).

If `3` of `20` reviewed items are confirmed dependent duplicates, confirmed duplicate rate in that reviewed set is `3/20=15%`. It does **not** prove exactly 15% of the entire suite is contaminated, nor how much any model score is inflated. Quarantine confirmed items and report unresolved candidates rather than subtracting a guessed “contamination correction.”

## 5. Scoring and reliability

Use deterministic programmatic scoring when the task contract permits it: exact normalized match, schema validation, reference execution in a sandbox or field-level checks. Define normalization before scoring; over-aggressive normalization can hide meaningful errors.

When correctness is open-ended, use a rubric with decision categories, allowed evidence, rater training/calibration, blinding, disagreement handling and audit sampling. An LLM judge may be a scalable measurement instrument, but its model/version, prompt/order, temperature, reference access, calibration against blinded human judgments and failure modes need documentation. It cannot validate itself.

Score invalid format, timeout, tool failure, refusal and judge failure explicitly. A re-run policy must be fixed before results: which failures are retryable, with what environment, and which count as failures. Selective retries after candidate outputs are seen bias comparisons.

## 6. Evaluation protocol and release gates

Run candidate and baseline on the same immutable manifest under matched tool/retrieval/sandbox state, prompt/template, decoding policy and resource constraints. Randomize or blind presentation where human/judge comparison is susceptible to identity or order bias. Preserve pairing by item.

Use gates in this order:

1. integrity: manifest, provenance, execution logs and candidate identity complete;
2. validity: coverage, scorer health, contamination review and required slice representation meet predeclared minima;
3. critical safety/correctness: zero-tolerance or bounded failure limits for named cases;
4. primary utility: comparison versus baseline with the declared uncertainty/non-inferiority rule;
5. operational: latency, cost, tool reliability and capacity fit;
6. governance: approvals, artifact registry, rollout and rollback readiness.

Do not average a critical security, safety or regulated-workflow failure away with thousands of easy successes. Equally, do not claim global failure from an unrepresentative anecdote; classify the scope and collect the required slice evidence.

## 7. Failure modes

| Symptom | Candidate explanations | Check | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| High aggregate, poor minority slice | Traffic-weighted aggregation hides a required domain | Inspect raw slices/counts and gate status | Block promotion or change scope; do not relabel aggregate as comprehensive |
| Candidate gain disappears on fresh data | Benchmark contamination or overfitting to suite | Fresh/dependency-separated holdout and provenance audit | Treat original claim as unvalidated |
| Score changes after harmless code change | Scorer/version/order/nondeterminism changed | Compare manifest, environment, seed and per-item traces | Pin harness; rerun matched comparison |
| Many items unscored | Tool outage, timeout, parser/scorer error | Report funnel and failure reasons by candidate/slice | Fix harness or count outcome per predeclared rule |
| Judge favors verbose answer | Rubric/prompt length bias, order bias | Blinded human calibration and length-controlled samples | Revise/validate judge; do not promote on judge score alone |
| Exact match says fail for equivalent output | Normalization/rubric too narrow | Error sample and alternative valid outputs | Improve contract/scorer version and rescore all candidates |
| Benchmark score improves but users regress | Benchmark population/mode differs from deployment | Population contract, shadow/controlled evaluation, incident review | Keep release gated; redesign suite |
| One public score drives decision | Construct coverage is narrow and known test exposure uncertain | Map suite tasks to deployment claims | Use it as one signal, not release proof |

## 8. Bounded benchmark decision exercise

**Scenario:** A candidate beats baseline on micro accuracy (`94/110=85.45%`) across two declared slices, but scores only `4/10=40%` on a regulated-workflow slice. Three of 20 reviewed suite items are confirmed dependent duplicates. The release policy requires at least 80% accuracy on the regulated slice and no confirmed duplicates in the final gate suite.

**Decision:** Do not promote. The candidate fails a hard slice gate and the final suite is invalid for the declared release decision. Quarantine duplicate items, construct/review a dependency-separated replacement slice, retain its provenance, then run baseline and candidate through the unchanged harness. The high micro score remains a descriptive result, not promotion evidence.

**Check:** Report coverage, duplicates, exclusions, item-level traces and all slice results. If no uncontaminated regulated evaluation can be assembled, constrain the deployment claim or keep the existing baseline; do not lower the gate after seeing the candidate result.

## 9. Interview and self-check questions

### Q1. Compute micro and macro accuracy for 90/100 and 4/10 slices.
**Answer:** Micro is `94/110=85.45%`; macro is `(.90+.40)/2=65%`.
**Reasoning:** Micro weights items; macro weights declared slices equally.
**Follow-up:** Which is the release statistic?
**Answer:** The predeclared one, plus any hard slice gates.

### Q2. Why is an item-level score not enough to reproduce a benchmark result?
**Answer:** Prompt, tools/assets, decoding, scorer, environment and candidate versions affect the outcome.
**Reasoning:** A benchmark is an execution protocol.
**Follow-up:** What preserves that protocol?
**Answer:** An immutable manifest and retained traces.

### Q3. What does 3 confirmed duplicates among 20 reviewed items establish?
**Answer:** A 15% confirmed rate in that reviewed set.
**Reasoning:** `3/20=.15`.
**Follow-up:** What does it not establish?
**Answer:** The global contamination rate or score inflation.

### Q4. Can a benchmark be relevant and contaminated?
**Answer:** Yes.
**Reasoning:** Relevance does not prove independence from development data.
**Follow-up:** Safe response?
**Answer:** Quarantine/rebuild the release gate and disclose limits.

### Q5. Why retain unscored items in reporting?
**Answer:** Excluding failures can bias coverage and candidate comparison.
**Reasoning:** The denominator is part of the claim.
**Follow-up:** What must be predeclared?
**Answer:** Retry and failure-treatment policy.

### Q6. When is an LLM judge acceptable?
**Answer:** As a versioned, calibrated measurement component with known limitations.
**Reasoning:** It is not ground truth by label alone.
**Follow-up:** What calibrates it?
**Answer:** Blinded human reference judgments and disagreement analysis.

### Q7. Why use a fresh/private holdout?
**Answer:** It can reduce exposure through known model-development paths.
**Reasoning:** It is stronger evidence against contamination in those paths.
**Follow-up:** Does it audit opaque pretraining?
**Answer:** No; disclose that limit.

### Q8. Can a candidate pass by high average if it fails a critical slice?
**Answer:** Not under a hard gate.
**Reasoning:** An average cannot override a defined critical failure.
**Follow-up:** What must change to release?
**Answer:** Candidate behavior/evidence or the product scope through governance, not post-hoc metric selection.

### Q9. What does comprehensive mean here?
**Answer:** Sufficient evidence for declared claims across required modes/slices and risks.
**Reasoning:** It does not mean every conceivable benchmark.
**Follow-up:** What makes it bounded?
**Answer:** A written population, decision and explicit exclusions.

### Q10. What promotes a candidate?
**Answer:** Complete provenance/validity, gates for critical and primary outcomes, operations and rollback.
**Reasoning:** A score without a valid protocol cannot authorize deployment.
**Follow-up:** What if the benchmark is invalid?
**Answer:** Do not promote; rebuild or narrow the claim.

## 10. Quick reference

| Question | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| What claim does this benchmark support? | Decision/population/unit/task contract and exclusions |
| Is aggregation defensible? | Predeclared weights, slice counts, coverage and hard-gate status |
| Is the suite independent? | Provenance, dependency split, audit scope/matches and unresolved limits |
| Is scoring reliable? | Scorer/rubric/version, exception policy and calibration/audit traces |
| Did C beat B? | Matched manifest/assets/decoding, paired item outcomes and uncertainty |
| Can C be promoted? | Integrity, validity, critical, utility, operations, governance and rollback gates |
