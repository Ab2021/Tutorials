# 9.4 — LLM-as-Judge and Human Evaluation

> **Course location:** Module 28. This lesson maps to `finetuning_course/Module_09_Evaluation_Debugging/9.4_LLM_as_Judge_and_Human_Evaluation.md` in [`Train/Templates/directory.txt`](../../../Templates/directory.txt). The original source body was unavailable; this is a new mapped draft.
>
> **Boundary:** A judge—human or LLM—is a measurement component, not ground truth. Validation must establish what its score means for a declared task population. For benchmark manifests and contamination, see [9.3](9.3_Evaluation_Benchmarks_Comprehensive.md).

## Learning outcomes

Learners can specify a decision rubric, blind and randomize comparisons, preserve human reference labels, calculate simple judge-human agreement, detect position/length/identity bias, route ambiguous cases to adjudication, and prohibit an uncalibrated judge score from promoting a model.

## 1. Define the claim before choosing a judge

A judge can score factual support, instruction compliance, safety, style, pairwise preference, tool trajectory quality or rubric-defined task success. These are different constructs. Define the unit, eligible population, prompt and candidate versions, available evidence, categories, allowed abstention, critical failure classes, tie rule, score aggregation and decision gate before seeing outputs.

A rubric must state observable criteria and counterexamples. “Helpful” is not a reproducible instruction. For a grounded answer, distinguish supported claim, unsupported claim, correct abstention, citation presence and citation entailment; do not collapse them into one opaque number if each drives a different product risk.

## 2. Experimental controls

- Blind candidate/model identity and randomize A/B order; balance each candidate's position.
- Freeze prompt, source context, tools, decoding policy, environment and reference evidence.
- Give judges/rater the minimum evidence required by the task; reference answers can help factual grading but can also anchor preference.
- Define timeout, malformed output, refusal, missing evidence and judge failure treatment before evaluation.
- Keep item-level rationale/trace subject to privacy policy; retain rater/judge version, rubric version and calibration set identity.
- Separate development/calibration examples from the final held-out evaluation set.

Repeated judging can estimate instability, but sampling the same biased rubric/prompt repeatedly does not validate it. Human reviewers can disagree for legitimate ambiguity; disagreement is evidence to classify, not noise to delete.

## 3. Calibration against blinded human review

For a binary pass/fail task, simple agreement is:

```text
FORMULA [JUDGE-01]: agreement = A / N
WHERE: A is the number of eligible items where judge and adjudicated human
       reference have the same decision; N is eligible compared items.
CONSTRAINT: both decisions use the same rubric/category mapping; N>0; reference
            review is blinded to candidate identity and independent of judge.
NUMERICAL EXAMPLE: agreement on 42 of 50 eligible calibration items is 42/50=.84.
INTERPRETATION: observed agreement is 84% on this sample and protocol. It does
                not establish agreement by class, domain, severity or future drift.
```

Agreement is insufficient when class prevalence is extreme: a judge that always says pass can agree often while missing every rare critical failure. Report confusion counts by critical category, per-slice agreement, unresolved/abstained rate, rater disagreement and the sampling unit. Use an adjudication process for disagreements and periodically refresh calibration after judge, rubric, candidate mix or task population changes.

For pairwise output review, report wins, losses, ties and invalids under an explicit policy. Do not convert an LLM judge's numerical “confidence” to probability without calibration evidence. If judges are used to rank systems, validate ordering stability under candidate order swaps, answer-length controls, prompt paraphrases and known adversarial examples.

## 4. Human evaluation design

Human evaluation needs rater eligibility, training, calibration, compensation/workload policy, conflict controls, accessibility/language coverage, privacy/security handling and escalation paths. Assign overlapping items to measure agreement and use blinded adjudication for material disagreements. A senior reviewer may resolve a case, but that decision changes the reference-label policy and must be recorded.

Sample by deployment-relevant unit. If multiple prompts belong to one customer/document/conversation, report that cluster; treating all prompts as independent can overstate precision. Oversample rare critical conditions deliberately, then report both targeted-slice and population-weighted results rather than blending them invisibly.

## 5. Failure modes

| Symptom | Check | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Judge favors first response | Swap A/B order on a balanced subset | Randomize/balance order; recalibrate before using results |
| Judge favors longer answers | Length-controlled paired audit | Revise rubric or score concise correctness separately |
| High agreement but missed critical errors | Category confusion matrix and rare-slice review | Add hard critical gate; do not rely on pooled agreement |
| Human raters disagree often | Inspect rubric ambiguity and evidence availability | Clarify rubric, train raters, adjudicate and report unresolved rate |
| Judge changes after model update | Re-run frozen calibration set by version | Version judge and block comparison until recalibrated |
| Reference answer anchors reviewers | Blind/withhold reference where task permits | Test protocol alternatives and record evidence exposure |
| Judge failure is dropped | Funnel of eligible, judge-failed and scored items | Predeclare failure handling and report coverage |

## 6. Bounded release exercise

A candidate receives 72% LLM-judge pass rate on 500 prompts. A blinded human calibration set has 42/50 agreement, but the judge misses 3 of 5 human-confirmed critical safety failures. **Decision:** do not promote. The pooled pass rate and 84% agreement cannot override critical miss evidence. Expand critical-slice human review, revise the rubric/judge prompt or limit the judge to triage; then rerun a frozen paired protocol with declared gates.

## 7. Interview checks

1. **What does 42/50 mean?** Observed 84% agreement under one protocol, not universal validity.
2. **Why blind model identity?** To reduce reputation/expectation bias.
3. **Why randomize pair order?** To detect and control position bias.
4. **Can high agreement prove safety?** No; inspect critical-class misses and coverage.
5. **Why preserve ties/invalids?** They reveal ambiguity or evaluator failure; discarding them changes the claim.
6. **What changes after a judge-model upgrade?** Recalibrate against frozen human references.
7. **What promotes a judged candidate?** Predeclared task, critical-safety, coverage, calibration and operational gates.

## 8. Quick reference

| Claim | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Judge score is usable | Versioned rubric/prompt, blinded calibration, slice/critical-error review |
| Pairwise result is fair | Same prompts, blinded randomized order, tie/invalid policy |
| Human result is reliable | Rater protocol, overlap, disagreement/adjudication and sampling unit |
| Candidate may promote | Held-out task and critical gates; judge score alone is insufficient |
