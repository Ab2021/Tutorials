# Financial Volatility Forecasting for a Risk Review

Evidence disclaimer: everything in this case is `ILLUSTRATIVE`. The return series is a constructed
simulation from a published-style variance recursion with a fixed seed, not a market record. No
named institution, instrument, vendor, limit breach, or trading result is represented, and no
number below is a measurement of any firm's performance. Where a figure is quoted it was recomputed
from the generating script described in Section 5e, so the arithmetic is checkable even though the
inputs are invented. Claims about how real markets behave are marked as assumptions, not findings.
`evidence_level: not-measured` means exactly that: the case teaches a method and its failure modes,
and its comparative numbers are properties of the simulated world, not of any traded asset.

## 1. The One-Line Framing

A risk manager needs one number each morning, a next-session conditional standard deviation for a
single exposure, and must choose between an exponentially weighted variance recursion and the
trailing-window average that the desk already trusts.

The decision under test is narrow: whether the review pack may print a candidate variance forecast
instead of the baseline, for a stated notional, without breaching a pre-agreed underprediction
guardrail. It is not a decision to trade, hedge, size, or recommend a position, and the forecast is
not a price view.

The framing that makes the case teachable is that the quantity being forecast, conditional variance,
is never observed. Every evaluation below therefore has to name which proxy it uses, and the case
shows the same comparison producing a 49.59% loss improvement against one proxy and a 0.52%
improvement against another. That gap, not the decay parameter, is the substance of the lesson.

## 2. Context & Background

A hypothetical risk function reviews a single equity-style exposure once per trading session. The
exposure is carried at a constant notional of 250 million currency units for the whole exercise, so
that every change in a risk number comes from the volatility forecast and never from a change in
position size. Holding the notional fixed is a teaching device: it isolates the forecast from
position management, which is a different discipline with its own controls.

The desk's existing pack prints a one-day 99% value-at-risk figure computed as a Gaussian quantile
of a volatility estimate, where the estimate is the root mean square of the last twenty eligible
session returns with an assumed zero mean. That construction is transparent, needs no fitting, and
can be reproduced by anyone with the price file. Its known weakness is arithmetic rather than
philosophical: twenty equal weights mean that a shock enters the estimate at full weight on the
session after it happens and then departs abruptly on the twenty-first session, while a long quiet
stretch drags the estimate down at the same fixed speed regardless of how much evidence has
accumulated.

The proposed candidate replaces the equal weights with geometric decay. A single decay parameter
governs how quickly the estimate forgets, and the whole method fits in one line of code, which is
why it is attractive to teams that must defend a number to auditors rather than to a research
committee. The case is built around the two questions that this simplicity raises. First, how is
the decay chosen without looking at the data the choice will be judged on. Second, what does a
better average loss actually license the desk to change.

Three organisational facts shape the rest of the narrative. Model risk oversight requires that any
number in the pack be reproducible from archived inputs, so the candidate must carry a manifest.
The limit framework is expressed in currency, not in standard deviations, so the forecast has to
survive a units conversion before anyone can act on it. And the vendor that supplies adjusted
closes occasionally restates history, which is the mechanism by which a plausible backtest can
become quietly optimistic.

## 3. The Business Question

The question the review pack has to answer is stated so that a wrong answer is visible: for the
next session, what conditional standard deviation should be printed, and is the candidate recursion
allowed to replace the trailing-window baseline under the desk's existing limit and the model-risk
policy's guardrails.

Decomposed, that is four sub-questions with four different owners. The statistician owns whether the
candidate forecasts better on data it never saw. The risk manager owns whether the resulting
currency figure respects the 10 million limit with a stated buffer. The data owner owns whether each
input was knowable at 07:30 local on the morning of issue. The model risk function owns whether the
selection procedure itself is documented well enough to be repeated by a reviewer who distrusts it.

Two formulations are explicitly rejected at the outset. A one-number price forecast is rejected
because the stakeholder asked about dispersion, and a point price prediction would answer a
different question with no bearing on the limit. A single realised squared return is rejected as a
target because it is one draw from the very distribution whose scale is in question; predicting it
directly optimises a noisy proxy and, as Section 6 shows numerically, can rank methods in the
opposite order to the variance itself.

## 4. Assumptions & Constraints

Each assumption below is stated with the numeric value it fixes, because an assumption without a
value cannot be falsified and cannot be audited.

| ID | Assumption or constraint | Value used | Consequence if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Exposure notional held constant | 250 million currency units | Risk numbers move for the wrong reason |
| A2 | Risk limit, one-day 99% | 10 million currency units | Headroom arithmetic in Section 9 changes |
| A3 | Return definition | log close-to-close, decimal | Percent and decimal units silently mixed |
| A4 | Conditional mean | zero, not estimated | Small bias, large variance saving |
| A5 | Tail model for the pack | Gaussian quantile, z = 2.3263 | Tail understated versus fat-tailed reality |
| A6 | Decision cutoff | 07:30 local on issue morning | Later data would be leakage |
| A7 | Eligible sessions | exchange calendar, halt days dropped | Calendar drift breaks horizon alignment |
| A8 | Vendor restatement window | up to 5 sessions after close | Revisions can enter lagged inputs |
| A9 | Evaluation origins | 1,499 sessions after a 700-session burn-in | Short samples cannot separate methods |
| A10 | Baseline | 20-session mean of squared returns | Window length is itself a choice, see Section 9 |
| A11 | Candidate | geometric decay, single parameter | One parameter may underfit regime structure |
| A12 | Guardrail | breach rate not significantly above 1% | A better average loss can still fail |

Constraint A5 deserves emphasis because it is the most consequential simplification in the pack. A
Gaussian quantile applied to a fat-tailed return distribution understates the frequency and the size
of extreme losses even when the scale forecast is perfect. The simulated series used here has an
excess kurtosis of 1.0419 and a skewness of -0.0659 over the evaluation window, so it is only mildly
non-Gaussian; a real equity series typically shows far more. The case therefore treats the Gaussian
tail as a documented limitation rather than a modelling achievement, and Section 12 shows the
specific check that separates a scale error from a tail-shape error.

Constraint A8 is the operational expression of a statistical hazard. If a vendor can restate a close
up to five sessions after the fact, then any backtest that reads today's price file to rebuild
yesterday's estimate has access to information that the desk did not have on the morning in
question. The magnitude is not small: with a decay of 0.94, the weight the recursion places on the
observation from five sessions ago is 0.06 times 0.7339, and the shock carried by that observation
enters the variance estimate at 0.044 of its squared size. A restated extreme return therefore moves
the printed volatility by a measurable amount for several weeks after the session it belongs to.

The constraints also include a hard operational one that has nothing to do with statistics. The pack
must be issued by 07:30, the vendor file lands at 06:40 on a good day, and the quality check takes
about twenty minutes. There is no room for a retry loop that re-downloads history, so a missing or
stale input has to produce an explicit deferral rather than a silently degraded number. Section 8
turns that constraint into a fallback rule with a recorded identity.
