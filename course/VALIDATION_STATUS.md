## 3. Claims boundary

The package may claim:

- It is a structured, industry-oriented curriculum covering supply chain analytics, forecasting, optimization, AI, and governance.
- It contains module-level learning outcomes, labs, assignments, rubrics, case studies, and sources.
- It is designed for self-study, university teaching, and corporate upskilling.
- It uses public, dated sources and labels uncertainty.

The package may **not** claim:

- That it is accredited, degree-granting, or professionally certified.
- That completion qualifies a person for a specific job or license.
- That its regulatory summaries are legal advice or compliance certification.
- That its benchmarks predict production performance.
- That its case studies prove a vendor's product works.
- That it has been validated by a learner cohort or external panel.

## 4. Regulatory content

Regulatory summaries are educational. They describe the position as of 27 October 2026 from public sources. They are not legal advice.

High-risk areas requiring professional review before use:

- EU AI Act classification and obligations.
- CSDDD due diligence scope and transposition.
- CSRD/ESRS reporting and assurance.
- CBAM declarant obligations and certificate calculations.
- DSCSA and EU FMD serialization compliance.
- Competition-law risk in pricing and information sharing.
- Customs classification, origin, and forced-labor import risk.

## 5. Dataset and benchmark content

- Dataset licenses are described at a summary level. Users must verify the current license before use.
- Benchmark results are historical and dataset-specific. They do not transfer automatically to a production supply chain.
- Some datasets are synthetic, anonymized, or collected for a different purpose. Their defects are noted where known.
- No proprietary or confidential data is redistributed.

## 6. Exit criteria for a validated edition

A future edition may be labelled "pilot-tested" when:

- At least one cohort of 10–25 learners completes the core modules.
- Pre/post competency assessments show measurable improvement.
- Lab completion and domain-project quality are reviewed against the rubric.
- At least three external practitioners review the track playbooks.
- Regulatory content is reviewed by a qualified professional.
- Known errors are corrected and logged.

A future edition may be labelled "externally reviewed" when at least two independent reviewers sign a review record with conflicts of interest disclosed.

## 7. Known limitations

- The course cannot substitute for supervised planning, legal, trade-compliance, or procurement experience.
- Case studies are selected for teaching value, not statistical representativeness.
- Market sizes, freight rates, and adoption percentages are dated snapshots.
- The program does not include a full production data platform or licensed solver environment.
- Some labs require datasets that must be downloaded and may change.
- The domain project depends on access to a real or realistic decision and data.
